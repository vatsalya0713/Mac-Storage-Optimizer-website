If you’ve come from Windows, you might be looking for **Disk Cleanup** — the tool that clears temporary files with a few checkboxes. macOS doesn’t have an app with that name, but it does have equivalents. Here’s how to do a proper disk cleanup on any Mac, including MacBook Air, MacBook Pro and iMac.

## Windows Disk Cleanup vs. what a Mac has

| Windows Disk Cleanup item | Mac equivalent |
|---|---|
| Temporary files | Cleared automatically on restart; app caches in `~/Library/Caches` |
| Recycle Bin | Trash — empty with <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>⌫</Kbd> |
| Downloaded program files | Old `.dmg` and `.pkg` installers in Downloads |
| Thumbnails cache | Quick Look cache (rebuilt automatically) |
| System error memory dumps | `~/Library/Logs/DiagnosticReports` |
| Windows Update cleanup | Handled by macOS; Time Machine local snapshots are purged when space is needed |

## Step 1: Use the built-in Storage settings

Open **System Settings → General → Storage** (on macOS Ventura and later; older versions: Apple menu → About This Mac → Storage → Manage). Wait for the bar to finish calculating, then work through the **Recommendations**:

- **Store in iCloud** — keep Desktop, Documents and Photos in iCloud and only download them when needed.
- **Optimise Storage** — removes watched Apple TV movies and shows.
- **Empty Trash Automatically** — deletes items 30 days after you trash them.

Below the recommendations, click the **ⓘ** next to each category (Applications, Documents, iOS Files, Mail…) to review and delete items. **Documents → Large Files** is the quickest win.

## Step 2: Clear old downloads and installers

Open **Downloads** in Finder, switch to list view (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>2</Kbd>) and sort by **Size**. Installers (`.dmg`, `.pkg`, `.zip`) are safe to delete once the app is installed.

## Step 3: Remove app caches and logs

Caches and logs are the Mac’s “temporary files”. In Finder choose **Go → Go to Folder…** and open:

- `~/Library/Caches` — move the folders of apps you use heavily to the Trash (quit them first).
- `~/Library/Logs` — old logs and crash reports.

Apps recreate what they need. Our [cache guide](/blog/clear-cache-mac) explains what is safe.

## Step 4: Uninstall apps you don’t use

In **Applications**, drag unused apps to the Trash. Many leave settings and caches behind in `~/Library/Application Support` and `~/Library/Containers` — remove those folders too, or use an uninstaller that finds them.

## Step 5: Find duplicates and large files

Storage settings don’t look for duplicate files, and they hide many large files inside folders. Use Finder search (<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>F</Kbd> → **File Size** → **is greater than** 1 GB) or a scanner — see [how to find large files](/blog/find-delete-large-files-mac).

## Step 6: Empty the Trash and restart

Space is only freed once the Trash is emptied. A restart then clears temporary and swap files. Check Storage again — it can take a few minutes to update.

## What not to “clean”

- Anything in `/System` or `/Library/Apple` — protected by macOS.
- Keychains and Preferences in `~/Library`.
- **Purgeable** space shown in Storage: macOS frees it automatically when needed, so you don’t have to.
