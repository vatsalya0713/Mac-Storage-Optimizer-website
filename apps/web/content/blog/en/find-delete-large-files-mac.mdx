A handful of big files often uses more space than thousands of small ones. These four methods find them, from easiest to most thorough.

## Method 1: Storage settings (easiest)

1. Open **System Settings → General → Storage**.
2. Click the **ⓘ** next to **Documents**.
3. Choose **Large Files** to see everything over a few hundred megabytes, biggest first. **Downloads** is listed there too.

Select files and click **Delete…** — note that this deletes immediately rather than using the Trash.

## Method 2: Finder search by size

1. In Finder press <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>F</Kbd> and set **Search** to **This Mac**.
2. Click the first pop-up (it says “Kind”) and choose **Other…**.
3. Tick **File Size** and click **OK**.
4. Set **is greater than** and enter a size, e.g. **1 GB**.
5. Right-click the column headers and add **Size**, then sort by it.

Click **Save** to keep the search in Finder’s sidebar for next time.

## Method 3: Terminal (thorough)

Terminal can see places Finder search skips. Every file over 1 GB in your home folder:

```bash
find ~ -type f -size +1G -exec ls -lh {} + 2>/dev/null | awk '{print $5, $9}'
```

The biggest folders in your home folder, largest last:

```bash
du -sh ~/* ~/Library/* 2>/dev/null | sort -h | tail -20
```

These commands only **list** files — they don’t delete anything.

## Method 4: Disk-space apps

- **GrandPerspective** (free) — a treemap where each file’s rectangle is sized by its space.
- **OmniDiskSweeper** (free) — folders sorted by size, to click down through.
- **DaisyDisk** ($9.99) — an interactive sunburst map; the trial scans but can’t delete.
- **MacDiskCleaner** (free to scan) — a Large Files list plus old files, downloads and duplicates, with each item’s size and location. Space Lens lets you browse folders by size.

## Where large files usually hide

- **Downloads** — installers, videos and zips you forgot about.
- **Movies** — screen recordings, exports and iMovie or Final Cut libraries.
- **`~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup`** — iPhone and iPad backups.
- **`~/Library/Developer`** — Xcode data and simulators.
- **Virtual machines and Docker** images.
- **Mail and Messages attachments** inside your Library.

## Before you delete

1. **Check the last-opened date** (Get Info, <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>I</Kbd>). Untouched for a year usually means safe to archive.
2. **Archive instead of deleting** anything you might need — an external drive is cheap.
3. **Use the Trash**, not instant deletion, so you can undo mistakes.
4. **Empty the Trash** to actually free the space.
