Open **About This Mac → Storage** and you'll often see "System Data" (sometimes labeled "Other") taking up an alarming amount of space — sometimes 50 GB or more. It's not one folder you can delete; it's a catch-all category covering several different things.

## What's actually inside System Data

- **Caches** — app, browser, and system caches
- **Logs** — crash reports and diagnostic logs
- **Time Machine local snapshots** — hidden backup snapshots stored on your internal disk
- **Spotlight index files**
- **Application support files** for apps you've installed, including ones you've since deleted

## Step 1: Check for Time Machine local snapshots

These are often the single biggest hidden chunk. Open Terminal and run:

```
tmutil listlocalsnapshots /
```

If you see snapshots listed, macOS is usually good about purging old ones automatically when space is needed — but if System Data is unexpectedly huge, this is the first place to look.

## Step 2: Clear caches and logs

This is the largest *controllable* part of System Data. Go to `~/Library/Caches` and `~/Library/Logs` in Finder (Cmd+Shift+G to jump there) and remove what belongs to apps you recognize and don't need cached data for.

## Step 3: Remove leftover data from deleted apps

Dragging an app to Trash on macOS does **not** remove its support files, caches, or preferences — those stay in `~/Library/Application Support` indefinitely. Over years of installing and removing apps, this adds up significantly.

## FAQ

**Is it safe to delete System Data?**
You should never delete the System Data category directly — there's no single folder for it. What's safe is clearing the caches, logs, and leftover app files that make it up, which is what the steps above (or a cleaner) actually do.

**Why does System Data keep growing back?**
Because caches and logs regenerate as you use your Mac. This is normal — it's why scheduled, automatic cleaning is more effective than a one-time manual clean.
