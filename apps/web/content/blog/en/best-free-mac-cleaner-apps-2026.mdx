A lot of apps marketed as "free Mac cleaners" are really free *trials* — you can scan for free, but cleaning anything requires paying. This list only covers tools where real cleanup works without paying anything.

## What "actually free" should mean

Before trusting a "free" label, check for:

- **No trial countdown** — a genuine free tier doesn't stop working after 7 or 14 days
- **Real cleanup, not just scanning** — some tools show you what's using space for free but lock the "clean" button behind a paywall
- **No forced account creation** just to use basic features

## The list

### 1. MacDiskCleaner — best overall free tier

Free tier includes junk cleanup (up to 2 GB per scan), a full duplicate file scanner with no limit, and a full large file finder with no limit — permanently, not a trial. ### 2. OnyX

Completely free, long-standing macOS maintenance utility (cache clearing, permission verification). No duplicate finder, no large-file scanner, and no guided "here's what's safe to clean" experience — it assumes you already know what each maintenance task does.

### 3. Photos.app's built-in Duplicates detection (macOS 13+)

Free, built into macOS, but only covers photos already inside your Photos library — not general file duplicates elsewhere on your Mac. See our [guide to duplicate photos on Mac](/blog/duplicate-photos-mac) for the full picture.

## What's usually missing from "free"

App uninstallers (that also remove leftover support files) and scheduled automatic scans are the two features almost every free tier holds back — reasonably, since they're the features power users actually pay for. If you only need junk cleanup, duplicates, and large files occasionally, a genuine free tier like MacDiskCleaner's covers that completely.

## FAQ

**Are free Mac cleaners safe?**
Yes, as long as they move files to Trash before permanent deletion rather than deleting outright — that's the standard every tool on this list follows.

**Do free Mac cleaners slow down my Mac?**
A well-built one shouldn't — MacDiskCleaner, for example, only runs when you launch a scan, with no persistent background process on the free tier.
