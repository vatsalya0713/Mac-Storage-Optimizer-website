"Your disk is almost full" is one of the most frustrating macOS alerts, mostly because it doesn't tell you *what* is full. Here's how to actually diagnose it.

## Step 1: See what macOS itself thinks is using space

Apple menu → **About This Mac** → **Storage** → **Manage**. This gives a rough breakdown (Apps, Documents, Photos, "System Data"), but "System Data" is usually the biggest and least helpful category — it's a catch-all, not something you can click into.

## Step 2: Find out what's actually in "System Data"

This is almost always some combination of:

- App caches and logs that accumulate silently over months
- Leftover files from apps you uninstalled without removing their support files
- Old iOS/iPadOS device backups
- Time Machine local snapshots

A scanner that actually enumerates these locations (rather than macOS's rough category estimate) gives you an actionable list instead of a mystery number. [MacDiskCleaner's Storage Overview](/#features) shows a real breakdown by folder and file type — not just "System Data: 41 GB."

## Step 3: Attack the biggest categories first

In order of typical impact:

1. **Large files** — old video exports, disk images, forgotten downloads. Often the single biggest recoverable chunk.
2. **Duplicates** — photos imported twice, files saved in multiple places.
3. **Junk** — caches, logs, temp files. Individually small, collectively significant.
4. **Uninstalled app leftovers** — dragging an app to Trash doesn't remove its `~/Library` support files.

## Step 4: Prevent it from filling up again

Storage doesn't usually get low from one big event — it's gradual, from caches and downloads accumulating over weeks. A scheduled scan (available in [MacDiskCleaner Pro](/#pricing)) running quietly in the background catches this before it becomes a "Mac is full" emergency again.

## How much space should cleaning recover?

For a Mac that hasn't been cleaned before, 20–50 GB is typical. If you've cleaned recently, expect a smaller amount from caches and logs that regenerated since.

## FAQ

**Why does my Mac say storage is full when Finder shows free space?**
macOS sometimes delays updating the reported free space after large deletions — restart, or wait a few minutes, before assuming the deletion didn't work.

**Is it safe to delete "System Data"?**
You can't delete that category directly — it's not one folder. What's safe is clearing the caches, logs, and leftover files that make it up, which is what a proper cleanup covers.
