“Your disk is almost full” rarely says **what** is full. Here’s how to diagnose it properly and get space back — fast first, thorough second.

## Free space right now (2 minutes)

1. **Empty the Trash:** <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>⌫</Kbd> in Finder.
2. **Quit apps you’re not using**, especially browsers with many tabs and video editors.
3. **Restart.** This clears swap and temporary files, which can be several gigabytes on a nearly full disk.

If you still have less than about 10 GB free, delete a few big files from Downloads before continuing.

## Step 1: See what macOS thinks is using space

Open **System Settings → General → Storage**. The coloured bar shows the main categories: Applications, Documents, Photos, iCloud Drive, Mail, Messages, macOS and **System Data**. Click the **ⓘ** next to each one to see and delete its contents.

## Step 2: Remove large files and old downloads

Under **Documents**, open **Large Files** and **Downloads**. Old installers, videos and archives are the quickest wins. Guide: [find large files on Mac](/blog/find-delete-large-files-mac).

## Step 3: Check iOS files and Messages

- **iOS Files** lists iPhone and iPad backups and software updates stored on your Mac — delete old ones.
- **Messages** can hold gigabytes of photos and videos; in Messages → Settings → General, set **Keep messages** to 1 year or 30 days if you’re happy to.

## Step 4: Deal with System Data

If **System Data** is very large, the usual causes are Time Machine local snapshots, device backups, developer data or a virtual machine. Read [why System Data gets so big](/blog/how-to-clear-system-data-mac) and the [step-by-step fix](/clear-system-data-mac).

## Step 5: Uninstall apps you don’t use

Under **Applications**, sort by size and remove what you no longer need, then delete the app’s leftover folders in `~/Library/Application Support`.

## Understand purgeable space

Storage sometimes shows **purgeable** space. That’s data macOS will delete by itself when it needs room — local copies of iCloud files, old snapshots and some caches. You don’t need to do anything about it.

## Keep it from filling up again

- Turn on **Empty Trash Automatically** in Storage recommendations.
- Move finished projects and old photos to an external drive.
- Keep **15–20% of your disk free** — macOS uses it for virtual memory and updates.
- Run a cleanup scan every month or two. MacDiskCleaner shows where space went since last time, and Pro can scan on a schedule.

## How much space should cleaning recover?

That depends on the Mac. One with years of downloads, device backups or developer tools often has tens of gigabytes to reclaim. A free MacDiskCleaner scan tells you the real number for your Mac before you spend time on manual cleanup.
