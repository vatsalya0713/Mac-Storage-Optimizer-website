If your Mac is running low on space, don't start with the small stuff — start with whichever of these gives you the biggest win first. This checklist is ordered by typical impact.

## 1. Find and remove large files (usually the biggest win)

Old video exports, downloaded disk images, and forgotten archives are often the single largest recoverable chunk of space. Sort your Downloads folder by size, and check `~/Movies` and `~/Downloads` specifically — these accumulate large files fastest.

## 2. Find and remove duplicate files

Photos imported twice, downloaded files saved in multiple places, duplicate PDFs — these add up without you noticing. A duplicate finder that hashes file contents (not just filenames) catches copies even when they've been renamed.

## 3. Clean junk files (caches, logs, temp files)

See our dedicated guide on [cleaning junk files on Mac](/blog/how-to-clean-junk-files-mac) — this alone commonly recovers several gigabytes on a Mac that hasn't been cleaned in a while.

## 4. Uninstall apps properly — including their leftovers

Dragging an app to Trash leaves its support files, caches, and preferences behind in `~/Library`. Over time, this "ghost data" from apps you removed years ago can add up to gigabytes. A proper uninstaller removes the app and its leftovers together.

## 5. Clear old iOS/iPadOS backups and unused disk images

If you've ever backed up an iPhone or iPad to this Mac via Finder, or mounted a `.dmg` installer without deleting it afterward, check `~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup` and your Downloads folder respectively.

## 6. Empty the Trash

Easy to forget — deleting a file doesn't reclaim the space until Trash is actually emptied.

## Do all six steps in one pass

Doing this manually across six different locations takes real time and requires knowing where to look. ## How much space should I expect to recover?

For a Mac that's never been cleaned, 20–50 GB is common. For a Mac cleaned regularly, expect a smaller but still meaningful amount from caches and logs that regenerate over weeks of normal use.
