“Junk” on a Mac isn’t one folder. It’s a mix of temporary data that apps and macOS create and rarely clean up. Here’s where it lives and how to remove it without breaking anything.

## Where Mac junk files hide

| Junk | Where | Safe to remove? |
|---|---|---|
| App caches | `~/Library/Caches` | Yes — quit the app first |
| Logs and crash reports | `~/Library/Logs` | Yes |
| Old downloads and installers | `~/Downloads` | Yes, once you’ve checked them |
| iPhone/iPad backups | `~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup` | Old ones, yes |
| Mail attachments | `~/Library/Containers/com.apple.mail/Data/Library/Mail Downloads` | Yes — they stay in the emails |
| Developer caches | `~/Library/Developer`, `~/.npm`, `~/.gradle` | Yes — tools rebuild them |
| Leftovers from deleted apps | `~/Library/Application Support`, `~/Library/Containers` | Yes, for apps you no longer have |
| The Trash | `~/.Trash` | Yes — that’s what it’s for |

Downloads, device backups and developer caches are usually the biggest; caches and logs add up over time.

## Option 1: Clean junk files manually

### 1. Clear app caches and logs

Quit your apps. In Finder press <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>G</Kbd>, open `~/Library/Caches`, and move the folders of apps you use heavily to the Trash. Do the same in `~/Library/Logs`. Apps recreate what they need.

### 2. Empty your Downloads folder

Sort Downloads by size and delete installers (`.dmg`, `.pkg`, `.zip`) for apps you’ve already installed, plus files you’ve already saved elsewhere.

### 3. Delete old device backups

Connect your iPhone or iPad, select it in Finder and click **Manage Backups** — or remove old backup folders from the MobileSync path above.

### 4. Remove leftovers from deleted apps

Look in `~/Library/Application Support` and `~/Library/Containers` for folders named after apps you no longer have.

### 5. Empty the Trash

Press <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>⌫</Kbd>. Nothing is freed until you do.

## Things that look like junk but aren’t

- **Language files inside apps** — removing them breaks the app’s code signature on modern macOS. Leave them.
- **`/System/Library/Caches`** — protected and managed by macOS.
- **Purgeable space** — macOS frees it automatically when needed.
- **Your Photos library** — it can be huge, but it’s your photos. Use the [Duplicates album](/blog/duplicate-photos-mac) instead of deleting the library.

## Is it safe?

Yes, when you stick to the locations above and keep things in the Trash until you’re sure everything still works. Avoid any tool that wants to delete system files, “optimise memory” or clean daily — macOS handles those jobs itself.

## How often should you clean junk files?

Every month or two is plenty for most people. If you’re a developer or edit video, check more often — build caches and render files grow quickly.
