"Junk files" on a Mac are the cache files, logs, temporary files, and leftover app data that build up silently over months of normal use. None of it is dangerous to remove — but finding it manually means digging through hidden folders most people never see.

## Where Mac junk files actually live

- `~/Library/Caches` — app and browser caches, usually the biggest single contributor
- `~/Library/Logs` — diagnostic logs most apps never clean up themselves
- `~/Library/Application Support` — leftover data from apps you've already uninstalled
- `/private/var/folders` — system-level temporary files

## Option 1: Clean it manually

1. Open Finder, press **Cmd+Shift+G**, and type `~/Library/Caches`.
2. Sort by size and look for folders belonging to apps you don't use — those are usually safe to empty.
3. Repeat for `~/Library/Logs`.
4. Empty the Trash afterward to actually reclaim the space.

This works, but it's slow, and it's easy to second-guess whether a given folder is safe to touch — a lot of these folder names aren't self-explanatory.
