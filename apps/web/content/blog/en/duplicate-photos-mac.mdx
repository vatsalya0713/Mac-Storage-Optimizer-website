Duplicates are one of the easiest ways to recover space safely: by definition, you still have a copy. The right tool depends on where they are.

## Why duplicates happen

- Importing the same photos from a phone, camera or card more than once.
- Downloading the same attachment several times (`file (1).pdf`, `file (2).pdf`).
- Copying folders to reorganise them and never deleting the originals.
- Syncing from several devices or cloud services into the same Mac.

## Part 1: Duplicate photos in the Photos app

Photos has a built-in duplicate detector (macOS Ventura and later).

1. Open **Photos**.
2. In the sidebar, under **Utilities**, select **Duplicates**.
3. Review each group, then click **Merge** for one group, or select several and choose **Merge Items**.

Photos keeps the best-quality version, combines albums, keywords and captions, and moves the extra copies to **Recently Deleted**, where they stay for 30 days.

**If the Duplicates album doesn’t appear:** Photos detects duplicates in the background, which can take hours or days after an update or a big import. Leave Photos open with your Mac plugged in.

**Note:** Photos only looks inside your Photos library — not at copies of the same pictures in Downloads, Desktop or an external drive.

## Part 2: Duplicate files everywhere else

Finder has no duplicate search, and searching by name is unreliable: renamed copies are missed and different files with the same name are flagged. Reliable duplicate finders compare **file contents** using a hash (a fingerprint of the data).

MacDiskCleaner works this way: it groups files with identical contents, shows each copy’s location, size and date, and pre-selects the extra copies while keeping one. Everything you remove goes to the Trash, so you can put it back. The duplicate scan is part of the free tier.

## Safe removal checklist

1. **Keep the copy in the place you actually use** (for example Documents, not Downloads).
2. **Don’t delete inside libraries** — Photos, Music, iMovie or Final Cut libraries manage their own files. Use the app instead.
3. **Skip app bundles and system folders.** Identical files inside apps are normal.
4. **Check cloud folders** (iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive): deleting there deletes on every device.
5. **Empty the Trash** only once you’re sure — that’s when the space is actually freed.

## Similar vs. duplicate photos

Duplicate means identical (or near-identical in Photos’ case). *Similar* photos — bursts, several shots of the same scene — aren’t duplicates, and deciding which to keep is a personal choice. Review those by hand in the Photos library.
